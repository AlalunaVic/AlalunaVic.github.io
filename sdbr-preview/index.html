Know your bow.

Eight parts decide how your bow feels on the string. Tap any part, on the bow or below, and watch it come apart: what it does, what it’s made of, and what Victor checks.

The head

The carved tip of the bow. The hair is anchored inside it with a small wooden plug, fitted into a hollow called the mortise. A protective plate covers its face.

What Victor checksCracks around the mortise. A cracked head is the most serious damage a bow can have, so it’s the first thing we look at, with you.

Make your rehair last.

Five habits that keep the hair gripping and the stick healthy. Swipe through.

Loosen it every time

Release the tension whenever you put the bow away. Hair left tight stretches, and the stick slowly loses its curve.

Keep fingers off the hair

Skin oil kills grip. Hold the bow by the stick and frog, never by the ribbon.

Rosin lightly

A few strokes is enough. Too much rosin makes the bow squeak and skate, and it’s easy to mistake for worn hair.

Watch heat and humidity

Hair shrinks in dry air and stretches in humid air. Never leave the case in a hot car or by a radiator.

Air out the case

Tiny “bow bugs” feed on hair in closed, dark cases. Open it and use the bow regularly; if hairs snap overnight, call us.
